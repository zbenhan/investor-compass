16 Personality Types: The Birth and Application of MBTI

Personality 2026-05-10

1. Origins and Development of MBTI

1.1 From Jung to MBTI

MBTI (Myers-Briggs Type Indicator) owes its existence to two American women: Katharine Briggs and her daughter Isabel Myers.

  • 1940s: Briggs and Myers began researching personality types
  • 1944: First version of MBTI officially released
  • Present: MBTI has become one of the most widely used personality tests globally

1.2 Theoretical Foundation of MBTI

MBTI is built on Jung's theory of psychological types, with one additional dimension added:

  • Extraversion (E) or Introversion (I): Energy direction
  • Sensing (S) or Intuition (N): How information is gathered
  • Thinking (T) or Feeling (F): How decisions are made
  • Judging (J) or Perceiving (P): Lifestyle orientation

2. Detailed Explanation of the 16 Personality Types

2.1 SJ Types (Guardians) - Traditionalists

SJ types typically value tradition, responsibility, and order.

  • ESTJ: Executive - Practical, organized, good at organizing
  • ISFJ: Protector - Responsible, thorough, loyal
  • ESFJ: Provider - Enthusiastic, compassionate, socially adept
  • ISTJ: Logistician - Practical, reliable, detail-oriented

2.2 SP Types (Artists) - Experiencers

SP types typically value the present moment, flexibility, and hands-on skills.

  • ESTP: Entrepreneur - Flexible, pragmatic, adaptable
  • ISFP: Adventurer - Sensitive, gentle, artistic
  • ESFP: Entertainer - Enthusiastic, outgoing, entertaining
  • ISTP: Virtuoso - Rational, flexible, analytical

2.3 NF Types (Idealists) - Communicators

NF types typically value possibilities, uniqueness, and personal values.

  • ENFJ: Protagonist - Charismatic, inspiring
  • INFJ: Advocate - Insightful, idealistic
  • ENFP: Campaigner - Enthusiastic, creative, inspiring
  • INFP: Mediator - Idealistic, loyal, understanding

2.4 NT Types (Rationalists) - Thinkers

NT types typically value knowledge, competence, and efficiency.

  • ENTJ: Commander - Decisive, leadership-oriented, strategic
  • INTJ: Architect - Independent, far-sighted, analytical
  • ENTP: Debater - Curious, intelligent, innovative
  • INTP: Logician - Rational, curious, theoretical

3. Career Matching for 16 Types

3.1 Typical Careers for Each Type

Understanding which careers suit different personality types can help us make better career choices:

Suitable careers for SJ Guardian types:

  • Administration, human resources, accounting, quality control
  • Military and law enforcement, education, healthcare

Suitable careers for SP Experiencer types:

  • Crafts, technology, sales, business
  • Sports, entertainment, food service, hospitality

Suitable careers for NF Idealist types:

  • Counseling, education, arts, writing
  • Religion, human resources, organizational development

Suitable careers for NT Rationalist types:

  • Science, engineering, technology, finance
  • Law, medicine, management consulting

3.2 Career Development Advice

Regardless of your type, the key to career development lies in:

  • Understanding your strengths and preferences
  • Choosing environments where you can leverage your strengths
  • Continuously developing relevant skills
  • Learning to supplement weaknesses when needed

4. Applications of MBTI in Interpersonal Relationships

4.1 Understanding Communication Style Differences

People of different types have distinct communication differences:

  • S types: Prefer concrete, clear information
  • N types: Prefer abstract, possibility-oriented discussions
  • T types: Prefer logical arguments
  • F types: Focus on emotions and values

4.2 Building Good Relationships

Understanding others' personality types can help:

  • Reduce unnecessary conflicts
  • Solve problems more effectively
  • Build deeper understanding
  • Appreciate the value of diversity

5. Limitations of MBTI and Its Proper Use

5.1 Limitations of MBTI

As a personality testing tool, MBTI also has its limitations:

  • Cannot completely define a person
  • Test results may be affected by current mood
  • Different test times may yield different results
  • 16 types may oversimplify human complexity

5.2 Using MBTI Correctly

MBTI should be used as:

  • A starting point for self-exploration, not a conclusion
  • A tool to understand others, not a label
  • A reference for career planning, not a decision
  • A starting point for personal growth, not an endpoint

6. Conclusion

MBTI provides a framework for understanding personality differences, helping us see that each of the 16 personality types has unique value and contributions. No personality type is superior or inferior; each has its own unique strengths and development potential. Understanding MBTI is not about labeling ourselves or others, but about:

  • Better knowing ourselves
  • Understanding and appreciating others' differences
  • Finding paths to development suited to us
  • Working more effectively with others
