Fundamental Analysis vs Technical Analysis: Opposites or Complementary?

Comprehensive Comparison 2025-12-20

In the investment field, fundamental analysis and technical analysis are two of the most commonly used analytical methods. For a long time, there has been fierce debate between supporters of these two methods, with each side believing the other has fatal flaws. However, in reality, these two methods have their own advantages and disadvantages, and in many cases, they can complement each other to help investors make more comprehensive investment decisions.

Core Differences Between Fundamental Analysis and Technical Analysis

Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis focuses on the intrinsic value of assets. It evaluates the true value of assets by analyzing factors such as a company's financial statements, industry prospects, and macroeconomic environment. Its core assumption is: prices will eventually return to value.

Representative figures of fundamental analysis include value investors like Warren Buffett and Benjamin Graham. They believe that by conducting in-depth research on a company's fundamentals, they can find undervalued high-quality assets, thereby achieving long-term stable returns.

Technical Analysis

Technical analysis focuses on the price trends and trading data of assets. It predicts the future price trends of assets by analyzing charts, technical indicators, and trading volume. Its core assumptions are: price movements reflect all available information, and history repeats itself.

Representative figures of technical analysis include technical analysts like Richard Dennis and John Murphy. They believe that by studying price trends and technical patterns, they can capture short-term market fluctuations, thereby achieving excess returns.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Fundamental Analysis

Advantages

  • Helps investors find truly valuable assets
  • Stable and reliable returns in the long run
  • Avoids being swayed by market sentiment
  • Suitable for long-term investment strategies

Disadvantages

  • Requires extensive financial knowledge and industry research
  • Cannot predict short-term price fluctuations
  • High information acquisition cost and poor timeliness
  • May ignore market sentiment and technical signals

Advantages and Disadvantages of Technical Analysis

Advantages

  • Can capture short-term price fluctuations and achieve excess returns
  • Low information acquisition cost and strong timeliness
  • Clear signals, easy to quantify and systematize
  • Suitable for short-term and medium-term investment strategies

Disadvantages

  • Cannot evaluate the intrinsic value of assets
  • Easy to generate misleading signals (false breakouts, false crossovers, etc.)
  • Lag, signals often appear after the trend has already formed
  • Cannot predict major fundamental changes

Why Can Fundamental Analysis and Technical Analysis Be Combined?

Although fundamental analysis and technical analysis focus on different aspects, they are not mutually exclusive, but can complement each other. This is because:

  • Fundamental analysis can help investors find valuable assets, while technical analysis can help investors choose the right entry and exit times
  • Fundamental analysis can help investors avoid being deceived by misleading technical signals, while technical analysis can help investors avoid falling into value traps
  • Fundamental analysis focuses on "what should happen", while technical analysis focuses on "what is happening". Combining them can provide a more comprehensive market view
  • The effectiveness of the two methods varies in different market environments. Combining them can improve the success rate of investment decisions

Three Practical Combination Methods

1. Fundamental Stock Selection, Technical Timing

This is the most commonly used combination method, with the core idea:

  1. Use fundamental analysis to screen a group of high-quality assets (companies with good profitability, financial health, and strong competitiveness)
  2. Use technical analysis (such as moving averages, MACD, RSI, etc.) to determine the best entry and exit times for these assets
  3. Take decisive action when fundamental and technical signals are consistent

The advantage of this method is: you can enjoy the long-term growth benefits of high-quality assets, while improving returns and reducing risks through technical timing.

2. Technical Stock Selection, Fundamental Verification

The core idea of this method is:

  1. Use technical analysis to screen a group of assets with good technical patterns (such as breaking through important resistance levels, bullish moving average arrangement, increasing trading volume, etc.)
  2. Use fundamental analysis to verify the intrinsic value of these assets, avoiding "junk stock speculation"
  3. Only invest in assets that are good both technically and fundamentally

The advantage of this method is: you can quickly capture market hotspots, while reducing investment risks and improving success rates through fundamental verification.

3. "Resonance" Strategy of Fundamental and Technical Analysis

The core idea of this method is:

  1. Look for assets where fundamental and technical analysis simultaneously send buy or sell signals
  2. Buy assets when both fundamental factors (such as positive company news, improving industry prospects, etc.) and technical factors (such as breaking through important resistance levels, increasing trading volume, etc.) are bullish
  3. Sell assets when both fundamental factors (such as negative company news, deteriorating industry prospects, etc.) and technical factors (such as breaking below important support levels, decreasing trading volume, etc.) are bearish

The advantage of this method is: more reliable signals, higher success rates, suitable for investors with lower risk appetite.

Practical Case: Apple Inc. Investment Analysis

Let's take Apple Inc. as an example to see how to combine fundamental analysis and technical analysis:

Fundamental Analysis

  • Apple is one of the largest technology companies in the world, with strong brand influence and ecosystem
  • Healthy financial situation, with continuous revenue and profit growth
  • Strong product innovation capability, with iPhone, iPad, Mac and other products leading in their respective fields
  • Rapid growth in service business, becoming a new profit growth point for the company

Technical Analysis

  • Apple's stock price has been in a long-term uptrend with a bullish moving average arrangement
  • During the 2020 pandemic, the stock price experienced a sharp correction, but then quickly rebounded, forming a "W" bottom pattern
  • Trading volume increases when the stock price rises and decreases when the stock price corrects, indicating good market sentiment
  • MACD indicator runs above the 0 axis, indicating a strong bullish trend

Combined Analysis

Through fundamental analysis, we know that Apple is a high-quality technology company with long-term investment value. Through technical analysis, we can find suitable entry times. For example, when the stock price corrected to near the 60-day moving average in 2020, it was a good buying opportunity; when the stock price rose sharply far from the moving average, we could consider taking partial profits.

Conclusion

Fundamental analysis and technical analysis are not mutually exclusive, but can complement each other. Investors should choose the analysis method that suits them based on their investment goals, risk tolerance, and time investment, or combine the two methods.

For long-term investors, they can focus on fundamental analysis supplemented by technical timing; for short-term investors, they can focus on technical analysis supplemented by fundamental verification; for most ordinary investors, it is recommended to adopt the "fundamental stock selection, technical timing" combination method, which can both enjoy the long-term growth benefits of high-quality assets and improve returns and reduce risks through technical timing.

Ultimately, the key to investment success does not lie in which analysis method you use, but in whether you can strictly execute your investment strategy, control risks, and maintain a rational investment attitude.
