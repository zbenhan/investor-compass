Four Psychological Functions: Thinking, Feeling, Sensing, Intuition

Personality 2026-05-10

1. Overview of Psychological Functions

Jung believed that there are four basic psychological functions (Four Psychological Functions) that serve as our fundamental ways of perceiving the world and making decisions. These four functions are divided into two groups: rational functions (thinking and feeling) and irrational functions (sensing and intuition). Everyone has all four functions, but one or two are typically the most developed and most frequently used, becoming our "dominant function."

2. Thinking Function

2.1 Characteristics of Thinking

The thinking function is a rational judgment function that understands the world through logical analysis. People with strong thinking function tend to:

  • Make decisions based on logic and reasoning
  • Pursue objectivity and consistency
  • Focus on cause-and-effect relationships and internal logic
  • Excel at analyzing problem structures and patterns

2.2 Advantages of Thinking Types

Thinking types typically excel in:

  • Analytical ability: Skilled at breaking down complex problems
  • Logical thinking: Capable of rigorous reasoning
  • Objective judgment: Not easily influenced by emotional factors
  • Systematic planning: Adept at building theoretical frameworks

3. Feeling Function

3.1 Characteristics of Feeling

The feeling function is also a rational judgment function that understands the world through subjective value assessment. People with strong feeling function tend to:

  • Make decisions based on personal values and emotions
  • Focus on the impact of decisions on others
  • Value harmonious relationships
  • Excel at understanding others' emotions and needs

3.2 Advantages of Feeling Types

Feeling types typically excel in:

  • Interpersonal sensitivity: Skilled at detecting emotions
  • Empathy: Able to understand others' perspectives
  • Value judgment: Capable of assessing subjective value
  • Conflict mediation: Skilled at reconciling differences

4. Sensing Function

4.1 Characteristics of Sensing

The sensing function is an irrational perception function that gathers information through the five senses. People with strong sensing function tend to:

  • Focus on concrete, observable facts
  • Value practical and present experiences
  • Rely on direct perception rather than speculation
  • Pay attention to details and specific information

4.2 Advantages of Sensing Types

Sensing types typically excel in:

  • Observation: Skilled at noticing details and changes
  • Practicality: Focused on feasible solutions
  • Memory: Good at remembering specific facts
  • Reliability: Down-to-earth and realistic

5. Intuition Function

5.1 Characteristics of Intuition

The intuition function is also an irrational perception function that gathers information through unconscious insight. People with strong intuition function tend to:

  • Focus on possibilities and potential developments
  • Enjoy exploring new ideas and concepts
  • Rely on sixth sense rather than five senses
  • Pay attention to future trends and developments

5.2 Advantages of Intuition Types

Intuition types typically excel in:

  • Creativity: Skilled at generating new ideas
  • Insight: Able to see connections between things
  • Imagination: Good at envisioning possibilities
  • Adaptability: Able to cope with change and uncertainty

6. Coordination and Development of the Four Functions

6.1 Dominant and Auxiliary Functions

According to Jung's theory, everyone has a "dominant function" (most developed) and an "auxiliary function" (second most developed). The combination of these two functions often determines a person's basic personality characteristics. For example:

  • Thinking + Sensing: Practical, analytical
  • Thinking + Intuition: Strategic, creative
  • Feeling + Sensing: Warm, caring
  • Feeling + Intuition: Idealistic, inspiring

6.2 The Importance of Developing Inferior Functions

While we have dominant and auxiliary functions, developing other functions is also important:

  • Contributes to well-rounded personal development
  • Enables better understanding of different types of people
  • Enhances ability to adapt to different situations
  • Promotes psychological balance and personal growth

7. Applications of Function Types in Real Life

7.1 Career Selection

Understanding your dominant function can help choose more suitable careers:

  • Thinking types: Suitable for analytical and logical work like engineering, analysis
  • Feeling types: Suitable for interpersonal work like counseling, teaching
  • Sensing types: Suitable for practical work like craftsmanship, accounting
  • Intuition types: Suitable for innovative work like entrepreneurship, invention

7.2 Teamwork

Understanding different function types of team members can:

  • Optimize task allocation based on individual strengths
  • Promote mutual understanding among members
  • Balance team diversity
